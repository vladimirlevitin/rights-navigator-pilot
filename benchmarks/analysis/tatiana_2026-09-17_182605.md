## Case-by-case operational deltas

### 1. Voluntary resignation after retirement age because of health

**Real problem:** the question contained two possible severance routes — retirement-age resignation and health-related resignation — and explicitly said that work began after retirement age.

**Defect found:** the original Navigator failed to extract `start_after_pension` and asked for a fact already present in the question. It also over-focused on the retirement route.

**Operational delta:** when health is the stated reason for resignation, first preserve evidence linking the medical condition to the need to stop this specific job, state the reason in the written resignation notice, keep the medical document and employer response, then verify Form 161 and actual severance deposits.

**Changes:**
- `severance-health-resignation` expanded into an action playbook grounded in section 6 of the Severance Pay Law.
- deterministic routing now gives the health-resignation rule priority when health is explicitly the reason for leaving;
- explicit extraction now recognizes that work started after retirement age.

### 2. 35% tax withheld from provident/pension withdrawal; disabled child; retroactive refund

**Real problem:** not “what is the tax rate?” but whether a past withholding can be recovered and which disability facts matter.

**Operational delta:** split the problem into two procedures:
1. determine whether the disability basis qualified for tax-free withdrawal in the relevant tax year;
2. if tax was already withheld, determine whether that year is still inside the six-year refund window and file the appropriate refund report.

**New playbooks:**
- `provident-withdrawal-relative-disability-75`
- `tax-refund-six-years`

**Intentional divergence from Tatiana:** the official Tax Authority material verified the route based on **permanent medical disability of at least 75%** of the applicant or a close relative, with onset after the first contribution to the provident fund; a child is a close relative. We did **not** encode the separate “188% disabled-child benefit” alternative because it was not established by the official material reviewed for this batch.

### 3. Refusing an offered public-housing apartment

**Real problem:** consequences depend first on which ministry manages the queue; an “offer refusal” is a process event, not simply an eligibility fact.

**Operational delta:** before refusing, identify the managing authority, ask whether the proposed refusal will count toward the official limit, keep the written offer/refusal record, check whether an exception applies, then obtain a written decision about the queue and increased rent aid.

**New playbooks:**
- `housing-aliyah-refusal-three`
- `housing-ministry-refusal-two`

Official procedures support different refusal limits and consequences for the Ministry of Aliyah and Integration and the Ministry of Construction and Housing.

**Not encoded:** the exact “two-room” allocation and the broad claim that a household may voluntarily leave one queue while retaining a particular increased cash benefit were not sufficiently verified for this exact scenario.

### 4. Woman born in 1966 transitioning from general disability to old-age benefit

**Real problem:** the decisive distinction is not simply “57% medical / 100% incapacity,” but whether she is an actual general-disability **benefit recipient immediately before old age**.

**Operational delta:**
1. determine retirement age from birth date;
2. verify actual receipt of the general-disability benefit in the month before transition;
3. use the special transition rule instead of treating the case as an ordinary first old-age claim;
4. if BTL asks for the ordinary qualifying period, check the exemption for a woman who received general disability immediately before old age;
5. compare the first old-age payment with the previous disability payment.

**New/refined playbooks:**
- `disability-to-old-age-transition`
- `old-age-disability-recipient-qualifying-exemption`
- `old-age-retirement-age-woman-1966`

**Extraction fix:** 57% medical disability and 100% incapacity are now extracted separately; the earlier parser could misread 57% as the incapacity rate.

**Intentional restraint:** we did not encode blanket claims about spouse salary, car ownership or absence of a new medical commission because the official sources reviewed for this batch did not establish those points broadly enough.

### 5. Missed increased rent assistance for previous rental months

**Real problem:** the recovery path depends on why the payment was missed: agency/data-transfer failure versus late/incomplete applicant action.

**Operational delta:** build a chronology from the entitlement decision, lease and submission receipts; request the official start date and month-by-month calculation; if two agencies should have exchanged data, request the transfer/receipt dates; then file a written recalculation demand and preserve the case number.

**New playbook:** `housing-rent-retroactive-differences`.

**Intentional divergence:** the official Housing Ministry procedure supports an ordinary retroactive difference of up to three months in the relevant calculation route. The “rare six months” statement from the Facebook answer was not encoded as a general rule. A proven agency-error dispute remains a separate escalation question rather than an automatic six-month entitlement.

### 6. Old-age benefit + unemployment; fear of debt after BTL review

**Real problem:** simultaneous receipt is only the first layer; the user needs to know what to do if BTL pauses, recalculates or creates a debt.

**Operational delta:** do not interpret a compatibility review as proof that the benefits are mutually exclusive. Check whether unemployment changed the income-tested old-age component; if a debt/pause appears, request a written month-by-month calculation and identify which component changed.

**Updated playbook:** `unemployment-old-age-concurrent-woman`.

### 7. Single parent with increased rent aid wants to open a small exempt business

**Real problem:** opening a business is not itself the decisive event; the current **basis of housing entitlement** and the applicable income test are.

**Operational delta:** find the current housing-entitlement certificate, identify whether entitlement is based on a specified BTL benefit or on income, verify the current threshold for this household, ask how self-employed income will be counted, update income data after starting activity, then check the next payment and request a written calculation if it changes.

**New playbook:** `housing-single-parent-income-test`.

**Intentional restraint:** the official page reviewed does not provide a complete formula for every form of self-employed income, so the Navigator does not invent one.

### 8. 300 unemployment days inside an existing entitlement window; return to work and later resignation

**Real problem:** users confuse the **bank of payable days** with the **calendar window in which those days may be used**.

**Operational delta:** always inspect both values separately in the personal account: remaining payable days and the calendar end date. A return to work may pause payment but does not by itself erase/restart the existing window. A later new claim must be checked through a new qualifying-period analysis rather than assumed from the old decision.

**New playbook:** `unemployment-entitlement-window-vs-days`.

### 9. Elderly cancer patient already receiving long-term-care hours; wants more hours and asks about general disability

**Real problem:** three different mechanisms were being mixed together: reassessment of long-term care, first filing for general disability around retirement age, and the interaction of long-term care with other benefits.

**Operational delta:**
1. for worsening functional condition, use the long-term-care reassessment route and keep the filing date;
2. determine the person’s position relative to retirement age before advising on a first general-disability claim;
3. distinguish **general disability** from **special services (שר״מ)** — they are not the same benefit;
4. general disability is not listed by BTL as one of the benefits that automatically requires choosing instead of long-term care, but its amount is counted as income in the long-term-care income test;
5. special services is a benefit for which BTL does require a choice against long-term care under the ordinary rule;
6. after any change, verify the new long-term-care decision and hours/services.

**New playbooks:**
- `long-term-care-worsening-reassessment`
- `disability-application-retirement-boundary`
- `long-term-care-benefit-interactions`

**Important correction to Facebook answer:** BTL states that a first general-disability claim may be filed **up to 12 months after retirement age**. Therefore the blanket statement “after retirement age you cannot apply” was not encoded. The Navigator now asks the decisive question: where is the person relative to retirement age?

### 10. 300 days exhausted before the displayed entitlement end date

**Real problem:** the later date in the personal account was interpreted as a promise of payment until that date.

**Operational delta:** explain and verify the two limits separately. If the entire bank of days has already been used, the later calendar deadline does not create more payable days; if payment was interrupted, unused days can only be resumed while the window remains open.

**Playbook:** `unemployment-entitlement-window-vs-days`.
